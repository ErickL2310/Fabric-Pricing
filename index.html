    Enter each yarn's share of the fabric by weight and its price delivered to the mill. Elastane, though a small share, often weighs heavily on price.

    Knitting

    Dyeing and wet processing

    Charges apply to the weight entering each process. Dark and reactive shades cost more than light ones. Cotton knits usually lose 4–8% in dyeing; enzyme washes add 2–4%.

    Finishing and printing

    Compacting, enzyme, softener, mercerizing, brushing, wicking, antimicrobial, coating. Printing is usually quoted per meter.

    Order costs and quality

    Fixed costs per order

    Fixed costs (lab dips, testing, setup, minimum surcharges) are spread over the order quantity, so small orders cost more per kilo.

      Machine and fabric

      Yarns and stitch length

      One row is enough when every feeder runs the same yarn at the same stitch length. Add a row when feeders differ: knit and tuck courses in piqué, or stripes in another yarn. "Feeders" is how many feeders of the repeat use that row.

      Stitch length is the yarn per needle, in mm. You can also type the length per 100 needles in cm: 27 is read as 2.70 mm.

      Fine-tune (optional)

      Scouring and bleaching usually remove 4–6% of a cotton fabric's weight, and an enzyme wash 2–4% more. Polyester loses about 1%. Greige fabric ignores this value.

      Weight and width move together. If the finisher sets the fabric wider than its natural width, it gets lighter by the same proportion and keeps more width shrinkage. Enter the width you will ask for and the weight follows.

      Calibrate with a real fabric (optional)

      Enter above the machine, yarns, stitch lengths and finish of a fabric you have already measured, then its real weight and width here. The app learns the difference and applies it to future estimates of the same structure.
